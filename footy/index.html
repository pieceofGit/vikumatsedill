Who has the rights

Iceland and the United States each have a single rightsholder per competition, so those answers are simple. Britain is the complicated one: the rights are split by kickoff slot, and once you know the slot — a UK slot, on the UK clock — you know the channel.

What counts as a big match

Starred rows are the ones worth clearing an evening for. Three things earn a star, and the most specific one is what the label says:

  • A named derby — Tyne–Wear, Merseyside, North London, Manchester, the West London and M23 ones, the Roses, El Clásico, the Madonnina.
  • A heavyweight tie — both sides drawn from the twenty or so clubs that decide these competitions.
  • Manchester United or PSG — flagged every time they play, whoever the opponent is.

Use ★ Big matches above to show only those. A scheduled job also pushes a phone notification each morning there is one on — every derby, heavyweight tie and Manchester United match, and PSG on their Champions League nights.

Icelandic time, British slots

Iceland stays on GMT all year round. The UK does not — from late March to late October it runs an hour ahead on British Summer Time. Kickoffs are published on the UK clock and the broadcast slots are UK slots, so this page works out the channel on UK time and then converts the kickoff to Icelandic time. Through the British summer each row carries a second, later time: that is the UK clock. Through the winter the two agree and the second line disappears.

Why some Saturday matches are on no British channel

UK broadcasters are not allowed to show any football live between roughly 14:45 and 17:15 on a Saturday. It is a long-standing rule meant to protect attendances in the lower leagues, and it is why a 15:00 Saturday kickoff in the Premier League shows Not on TV against the UK. In Britain those matches turn up as highlights on Match of the Day on BBC One later the same night.

It is a British rule only. Sýn show those matches in Iceland like any other, which is why a blacked-out row still lists a channel here.

Why later matches say “not selected yet”

Broadcasters choose which matches they want roughly five to six weeks in advance, and the kickoff times move when they do. Anything beyond that window is shown here on its provisional Saturday-afternoon slot, with no channel attached, because no channel has picked it yet.

What changes after this season

2026/27 is the last year of both current deals. From 2027/28 Paramount takes over TNT Sports' Premier League package alongside Sky, and Paramount+ takes the majority Champions League package. Amazon Prime Video keeps its Tuesday first pick in the Champions League through to 2030/31. The slot mapping on this page will need revisiting next summer.
